# Welcome to thisisgenaro.com

Hi, and welcome to **thisisgenaro.com**.

I'm an IT Infrastructure Specialist, and I built this site as a place to share the things I learn, observe, question, and apply through my work.

You'll find material around infrastructure architecture, troubleshooting, operations, observability, incident analysis, and the practical decisions involved in keeping systems running.

But infrastructure work is rarely just about technology.

Knowing how to configure a platform or troubleshoot a failure matters. So does being able to communicate with the person whose work has suddenly stopped, explain uncertainty during an incident, or translate a deeply technical problem into information that management can actually use to make a decision.

I believe both sides belong to the same profession.

## Purpose

This site is a space for exposing thoughts, experiences, and engineering reasoning.

Some of the situations presented here are based on patterns and experiences encountered throughout real infrastructure work. Rather than making the people, companies, or circumstances behind those experiences the subject, I reconstruct them inside a fictional world.

The purpose of that abstraction is simple:

**keep the focus on the why, what, and how—not the who, where, or when.**

Organizations, people, timelines, and surrounding circumstances may therefore be fictionalized or reconstructed. The technical problems, operational behaviors, trade-offs, and lessons are what matter.

Not every story will have a perfect environment, either.

There will be legacy systems, incomplete documentation, constrained budgets, technical debt, questionable decisions, missing visibility, and sometimes people trying to make the best decision they can with incomplete information.

In other words, infrastructure as it usually exists in the real world.

## The Site

The site is organized around three connected spaces.

### The Journal

**The Journal** is the more open and personal side of the site.

It's where I write about ideas, observations, lessons, and opinions related to infrastructure and operations.

It is also where incidents can begin before all the answers are known.

An entry may start with a symptom, follow an investigation as new evidence appears, or return to an incident afterward to explore what it taught us.

The Journal keeps the **thinking** visible.

### The Incident Library

**The Incident Library** is the operational record.

Here, incidents are reconstructed more formally: symptoms, impact, evidence, hypotheses, dependencies, timeline, root cause, recovery, and lessons learned.

The written record is accompanied by graphical and interactive views that make it possible to explore different stages of the analysis and examine individual pieces of evidence in context.

Where the Journal can ask:

**What are we seeing? What might this mean?**

The Incident Library eventually has to answer:

**What actually happened, and what evidence supports that conclusion?**

### The World

**The World** provides the setting.

It contains fictional organizations and people designed to resemble the kinds of environments found in real IT operations: logistics companies, healthcare organizations, financial institutions, manufacturers, service providers, and others.

These organizations have their own infrastructure, limitations, histories, dependencies, technical debt, and people.

Over time, incidents will become part of that history.

The intention isn't to build fiction for its own sake. It is to give technical situations enough context to feel like they belong to a living environment rather than an isolated diagram.

## One more thing

I don't expect everything published here to present engineering as a sequence of perfect decisions.

Troubleshooting rarely works that way.

Sometimes the first hypothesis is wrong. Sometimes it is reasonable but incomplete. Sometimes a system behaves exactly as designed while producing an outcome nobody expected.

Those moments are often where the useful lessons are.

So this site is as much about documenting **how we arrive at an answer** as it is about documenting the answer itself.

If something here gives you a new troubleshooting idea, helps you recognize a pattern in your own environment, makes an architectural decision easier to explain, or simply causes you to look at a familiar problem differently, then the site is doing what I built it to do.

Welcome.

— **Genaro Hernández**
